# The Minister/Preacher persona

## What it is

A private self-check for someone who prepares and delivers messages to a
congregation. It asks where AI is serving the calling, and where it may be
quietly substituting for the work God does in a preacher through prayer, study,
dependence, and presence with people.

It follows the same path as the other personas: the reading appears after an
email address is given, and a PDF arrives in the inbox. The record is kept with
the rest, and treated with more care than the rest.

## The thesis

A sermon can get smoother while a preacher gets thinner.

Output can rise while prayer, study, memory, and presence quietly recede. From
the inside the two look the same for a long time, and the congregation may not
notice for longer. This check exists to tell them apart early, privately, and
kindly.

## The theological frame, and where it comes from

The instrument does not invent a theology of technology. It embodies the one set
out in the documents it was built from.

- **Technology answers a fallen world.** Spencer reads the fig leaves of Genesis
  3 as the first remedial technology, and shows human capacity at its fullest
  when exercised in dependence on God. The question for any tool is whether our
  response through it reflects dependence on God or an attempt at independence
  from him.
- **Attention is a theological act.** The fear of the Lord, Spencer argues, "isn't
  about terror. It's about attention." Where a preacher's attention goes in the
  study is already a matter of worship.
- **Friction can be holy.** Faith at Work names the danger of cognitive debt and
  calls for "the deliberate recovery of friction". The practice it commends is
  read first, think first, write first, then use AI for feedback.
- **Some things are not outsourced.** Faith at Work draws the line plainly: AI may
  serve administration, scheduling, editing, and communication, and not prayer,
  spiritual discernment, writing the sermon, counseling, or presence with
  suffering people. "The church needs faithful shepherds who have listened to God
  and to their people. Ministry is not content production."
- **The task is wisdom, not information.** If preaching were only the transmission
  of facts, a machine could do it better. "An AI cannot preach from a place of
  brokenness, repentance, and the ongoing experience of the Holy Spirit." Todd
  Korpi, quoted in the same script: "AI deals in knowledge, humans deal in
  wisdom."
- **Truth does not transfer to the machine.** AI "can invent sources, distort
  meaning, and repeat falsehoods", and "if I use AI to write something, I remain
  responsible for whether it is true."
- **The test is neighbor-love.** If a tool helps a pastor draw near, it may serve
  love; if it helps them avoid, it has become a problem.

## The context, cited and not overstated

Barna Group's 2026 State of the Church research with Gloo found that only 13
percent of United States Protestant pastors do not use AI at all, that half use
it for brainstorming and about a third for biblical or theological research, and
that 24 percent now write or edit sermons with its help, roughly double the 12
percent comfortable doing so in early 2024. At the same time 71 percent describe
themselves as cautious and 40 percent as conflicted. Separate Barna research
found about a third of practicing Christians rate spiritual advice from AI as
good as advice from a pastor.

The instrument treats none of these numbers as a norm to match. It treats the
caution most pastors already feel as wisdom worth equipping.

## The ten readings

| Name | What it measures | The goal it points toward |
|---|---|---|
| Authorship Before God | Whether the message is received and owned rather than accepted | You can stand behind every claim in your own words |
| Faithfulness to the Text | Whether AI-supplied material is checked before the pulpit | Nothing is preached that you have not confirmed |
| Unaided Preaching Capacity | What remains if the tools are gone | You would still have a word from the Lord for Sunday |
| Ministry AI Fluency | Knowing what the tool is for, and what it is not for | The tool sits in its right place |
| Formation of the Preacher | Whether study stays in you | What you learned is still in you weeks later |
| Deeper Study | Whether the tool deepens the exegesis | Your study goes further, and you can say how |
| Craft of Preaching | Whether the craft is still growing | Better than a year ago, not only faster |
| Discerning Practice | Whether the practice is examined and rested from | Reviewed, not merely repeated |
| Integrity and Care | Honesty, confidences, presence | Your people could learn how you use AI and trust you more |
| Voice and Witness | Whether the sermon is yours | Recognisable with your name removed |

Reported composites: Formation Health Score, Pulpit Integrity, Dependence
Exposure, Retained Formation, Study Depth, Ministry Readiness, Exposure Gap.

## How to read a Preaching Formation Check

**Start with the opening.** The archetype names a pattern, not a person, and each
one ends in hope with somewhere to read. The disclaimer sits at the top rather
than the bottom on purpose.

**Then the two lists.** Where AI is serving the calling, and where it may be
standing in for the work. An empty list on either side is a real result.

**Then the Dependence Check.** This is the one to sit with. It reads where prayer
and the text sit in the week, and it is a mirror rather than a measure: it does
not touch the score, the stage, or the archetype, and the two questions behind it
are never stored.

**Then the outage reading, and faithfulness.** What would happen if every tool
vanished this week, and whether what reaches the pulpit has been checked.

**Then the way forward.** One thing this week, a rhythm this month, the slower
work of a season, each with what it would look like if it were happening. These
are offered, not prescribed. So is the reading list.

## What is kept, and what may be shared

The record is kept: name, email, the reading, and the same context every other
submission carries. It was built anonymous and changed at the owner's decision,
and the admin carries a standing note about handling it, because a minister
answering honestly about prayer and dependence has said something more exposing
than anything else this instrument collects.

What may leave that record is narrow and tested. The prepared social posts say
that the check was taken and, where the answers support it, that the practice met
the standard it looks for. They carry no score, no stage, no archetype, and no
dimension. The suite asserts this on both a strong and a weak reading, so a post
cannot start leaking findings through a later edit.

The standard is met when authorship, verification, and integrity all sit at 55 or
above, unaided capacity at 45 or above, and no harm pattern has fired. Anything
short of that shares as completed, with nothing about what was found.

The wording stops short of certifying anyone's use of AI as safe, and says so on
the page. This instrument reads self-reported habits. It can say a practice met a
standard of responsible use on the day it was taken; it cannot certify safety,
and a claim it cannot support would be worth less than the one it can.

## What it is not

It is not a measure of anyone's faithfulness, calling, or ministry. It sees
habits, not hearts. It assumes no denominational norm, and a settled conviction
against using these tools in preaching is treated as a formed position that
reaches the same stages as any other, never as a gap to be closed.
