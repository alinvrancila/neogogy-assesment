# Deliverables O and P: Limitations, Self-Critique, and Validation Plan

## What this instrument can and cannot infer

It measures self-reported behavior and situational judgment, so it can describe patterns a person's answers are consistent with; it cannot establish causality (that AI caused a change), verify behavior (that verification actually happens), or diagnose anything. Reports use hedged language throughout and are labeled as assessment indices, not validated measures. Three inputs per construct sits below the conventional floor for a stable subscale; per-dimension confidence levels exist partly to keep that honest. Social desirability remains partially gameable: a respondent who consistently fakes claims, reverses, and behaviorally anchored scenarios will beat the consistency check; the design raises the cost of faking rather than eliminating it. Outcome items rely on retrospective self-comparison, which is noisy. The intentional-selectivity determination rests on one branch answer plus judgment scores and should be treated as a hypothesis about the respondent, which is how the Grounded Selectivist narrative words it.

## §61 self-critique, applied

The redesign was checked against the spec's own critique list before completion. Enthusiasm is not rewarded: usage never adds score, and daily-use uncritical respondents are gated below weekly-use verifiers (tested, cases A/B). Thoughtful non-use is not penalized: intentional selectivity reaches stage 8+ (tested, case D and synthetic 2). Productivity and learning are separated: fluency and transfer are distinct constructs and their divergence fires a dedicated harm pattern (tested, cases E/F). Confidence and competence are separated: B2 calibration measures whether self-prediction tracks measurement. High continuum levels cannot be reached over severe weaknesses: gates, tested. Advice derives from responses: tested. Personas are genuinely different: tested. Certainty is not fabricated: missing evidence degrades confidence and legacy rescoring marks unmeasured dimensions preliminary, both tested. Two known residual weaknesses are recorded honestly: the archetype rule order embeds judgment calls that only pilot data can arbitrate, and the developmental-index weights are theory-driven priors awaiting calibration.

## Validation plan

Phase 1, pilot (first 300 to 500 respondents): item-level distributions, straight-lining and completion-time screens, internal consistency per construct (with the caveat that three items bound what alpha can show), and claim-scenario correlation per pair to identify items where the pair measures different things.

Phase 2, structure (500 plus): exploratory factor analysis against the 10-construct model; collapse or split constructs the data rejects; re-estimate the developmental-index weights from data rather than priors; check measurement invariance across the four personas before comparing them.

Phase 3, criterion (requires partners such as LCI classes): compare student profiles against unaided assessment performance across a term (the transfer and dependency constructs make directional predictions that can be falsified); compare teacher profiles against classroom AI-policy audits.

Phase 4, longitudinal: retest at 3 and 6 months; stage transitions should track the reported behavior changes from the roadmap, and the calibration gap should shrink on retest if the report is doing formative work.

Every phase is cheap to act on because raw answers are stored: rescoring under revised weights, thresholds, or construct structure is a batch job, not a re-survey (see MIGRATION.md).

## The Business Owner persona

Everything above applies, and three caveats are specific to this persona.

**One person answers for a whole business.** Every other persona asks someone
about their own practice, which they are in the best position to know. This one
asks an owner about an organisation: what staff do, what data moves, what would
happen during an outage. The answers are still self-report, but they are now
self-report at one remove, and the gap between what an owner can see and what is
happening is real.

**Governance results should be read as a lower bound of exposure.** Research
through 2026 consistently finds that executives overestimate their visibility
into staff AI use by a wide margin, and that unsanctioned tool use is the norm
rather than the exception. A business scoring well on Governance, Data, and Trust
has answered that it has a policy and knows what is in use. It has not
demonstrated that the policy is followed. The report says this in the dimension
content rather than leaving it to be inferred.

**Not legal, financial, or compliance advice.** The register names categories of
exposure, including legal and compliance ones, because an owner needs to know
which kind of problem they are looking at. Naming a category is not a legal
opinion, the instrument has no view of the jurisdiction, the contracts, or the
regulator involved, and the disclaimer on every business report says so.

**What the persona deliberately does not claim.** It does not price exposure, it
does not estimate probability, and it does not benchmark one business against
another. Severity in the register reflects how the answers landed, nothing more.
